## I am a web developer

Say that to yourself. Say it three times over if you have to.

Get used to it.

If you have built things for the web; and plan on continuing, congratulations because you're a web developer. There is no solid point in time or badge you acquire that suddenly means you're official. There is no library, framework, or method you need to master before this happens, it just, is. Nobody gets to tell you if you're a developer or not.

It's not about what you've **done;** it's about what you **do.**

### You're not a developer because you don't do X and Y, or use Z

This one might come up here and there, some developers get caught up in a specific language or framework that they think it's the only way to program. To them, anything else is mere amateur hogwash.

It could also rear its head in the form of, "X language is shit," or "Just stop, you're wasting everyone's time."

In an excellent post by <Link href="https://medium.com/@d4nyll/the-open-source-community-have-no-place-for-disrespect-70c85d473332">Daniel Li</Link> about disrespect in the open source community, he calls out Linus Torvald himself (creator of the Linux kernel) on the issue. Maybe the *leaders* in the community could set an example…?

### You're not a developer because you haven't released anything

This one is personal for me.

I recently released a project I had been working on for a few months, and afterward, I did have this buzz feeling for a few days. I couldn't put my finger on it at first, but I've since identified it as my "AHA" moment.

After releasing my Jekyll theme as a gem and throwing everything up on a repo, there was this massive sense of relief, but was I any more of a web developer afterward?

I felt good, but nothing had changed. However, I am much more comfortable calling myself a web developer now that it's there on GitHub for the world to see.

*Feel free to check out my <Link href="https://github.com/jpasholk/jekyll-uikit">project</Link> on GitHub if you'd like!*

There will always be a random stranger on the internet who, for whatever reason, is downright nasty. No one is safe from their scrutiny, save themselves.

At some point, after releasing something to the public, there will be someone who says some straight up rude things about your work, and perhaps you as well. It's just something that we have to deal with in modern society. Anonymity on the internet has brought out the worst in us in a lot of ways.

When confronted with these types of people, it's best to remember one thing; **don't** feed the **trolls**.

## Imposter Syndrome

From Wikipedia;

> <Link href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impostor_syndrome">Impostor syndrome</Link> (also known as impostor phenomenon, fraud syndrome or the impostor experience) is a psychological pattern in which people doubt their accomplishments and have a persistent, often internalized fear of being exposed as a "fraud".

Originally coined in the late seventies, Imposter Syndrome is a plague to the development community. Perhaps it is the nature of the industry, in which there isn't any accepted external evidence that demonstrates one's competence. Or that there are so many barriers to entry; and many curtains to pull back.

### Confidence and Competence

> "Ignorance more frequently begets confidence than does knowledge." — Darwin

Darwin made the connection between confidence and this phenomenon years before anyone had considered it. Confidence is a crucial factor for success. One must have the courage to move forward and complete a task even if they don't have all the information.

*Does it matter where you derive your confidence?*

As long as you're still moving forward — and not making massive critical mistakes — does it matter? It might not matter as much as with other topics; the main thing with coding is that you keep learning.

If you're familiar with the feeling of waiting for those around you to "find you out," it might be helpful to consider what type of impostor you are so you can problem-solve accordingly.

## What can be done?

The most important thing you can do is recognize what is causing you to lose confidence and feel inadequate.

Is it a new job or project? Or a new technology that everyone seems to understand but you? Maybe it's because you're not 'getting it' as fast as you'd like? Whatever is it, it's your job to identify what exactly is tripping you up and do something about it; there is no one else who will.

### Reach out to someone

By selecting someone who isn't a stakeholder, they can help you realize when you're irrational and bring you back to reality. This person might even be able to remind you of your past achievements or something else that lifts your spirits.

The point is to get back on track and avoid being bogged down by your worries.

### Realize you are not alone

While it may seem like it, you aren't the only one. There are more people in your shoes than you would imagine, and they are just as terrified as you. Not only that, but there have been many more that went through it and survived.

Another thing to consider after being hired — and still feeling like a fraud — is the people who helped get you the job. The hiring manager, the HR person, and your boss all made choices based on what they saw on your resume, and what they saw in person. You didn't fool them; you're not *that* clever.

## Final Thoughts

Imposter syndrome is very real, and it's a plague to many. I hope that by writing this post, I can overcome my fears of being a charlatan, maybe help a few others do so as well. Remember, this is a community, and there is room for all shapes and sizes of developers. Let us start conversations, not conflicts.
